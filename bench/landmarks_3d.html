cIIIs04 tract landmarks

A healthy adult (MASiVar cohort III, subject 04), focused on three tracts used in surgical planning: the corticospinal tract, the arcuate fasciculus and the uncinate fasciculus. The tractography pipeline ran 17 times on one scan: once as acquired and 16 times on copies with its noise drawn again. The same subject was scanned three more times, and each of those scans got 17 runs too. Shells show how much of a tract the runs agree on; patches show where a real repeat scan put the tract outside the envelope; fans show single streamlines drawn from all 17 runs and the repeat scans. Brain anatomy from FastSurfer, a segmentation of the subject's T1 image, is drawn with the tracts and used to check where each streamline ends.

Research prototype. This shows how reproducible the pipeline's output is, and a rough check of its streamlines' ends against automated anatomy labels, not where the fibers truly run. The internal-capsule sites come from the FastSurfer anatomy; the other sites were placed from the tracts themselves, so their names are approximate. Not for clinical use.

Reading the view

Drawn from the data stores: the shells and the anatomy are read in your browser from two data files (the ensemble's and FastSurfer's), not from precomputed surfaces. All surfaces are traced together along each line of sight, so a nearer surface always covers a farther one.

Present: a run has a tract in a 1 mm voxel when the tract's streamlines pass densely enough there: at least 0.3 streamlines per square millimeter, smoothed over 1 mm (about two streamlines through a 2 mm voxel).

Core: voxels where the tract is present in at least half the runs. Envelope: the voxels with the most runs agreeing, down to the count where they hold the stated share of all the runs' presence. Widest: any run. Cloud: opacity follows the share of runs with the tract present.

Stated and held: a stated level is not a promise. The note under Envelope gives how much of another scan's tract each level held when each of this subject's four scans was left out in turn.

X-ray surfaces: every surface is nearly clear where it faces you and solid at its edges, so nested shells read as outlines. "Core: solid" draws the core opaque to show its shape; fans hidden by it then show as faint ghosts.

Patches: where the compared scan's own run has the tract but the envelope does not, in a lighter shade of the tract's color. Clusters may mean the envelope is too tight, a registration error, or a real difference between scans.

Fans: each seed near the site drawn once per run, colored by the tract that run gave it (or by that run's second choice), with the rescans' versions in white. Each site's fan colors are listed with the site.

Endpoint check: each run's version of a streamline in blue if its two ends fit its bundle's anatomical rule (for example, the CST from sensorimotor or premotor cortex to the brainstem), orange if not, gray if its bundle has no rule. The rules are a first pass, not checked by an anatomist.

Anatomy: FastSurfer's most likely label per voxel, on the same 1 mm grid as the shells, aligned to the diffusion scan.

Limits

One healthy subject, one scanner (Philips, 2.1 × 2.1 × 2.2 mm, b = 1000 and 2000). The rescans were registered rigidly and tracked on the reference grid. Shells are surfaces of vote counts smoothed slightly (0.6 mm); their thresholds set the apparent boundary. The held-out figures come from this subject's own four scans, not from other subjects. Coordinates are scanner millimeters in the patient's frame (R/L, A/P, S/I labels).
