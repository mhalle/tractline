PAT16 tracts in 3D

Tracts around a temporal tumor from 17 runs of the tractography pipeline: one on the scan as acquired and 16 on copies with its noise drawn again. Shells show how much of a tract the runs agree on; fans show the 17 versions of single streamlines near the tumor, so you can see where they stay together and where they split.

Research prototype. This shows how reproducible the pipeline's output is, not where the fibers truly run. Nothing here was checked against stimulation mapping or histology. Not for surgical planning.

Reading the shells

Shells are drawn within 25 mm of the tumor.

Core: voxels the tract holds in at least 9 of the 17 runs (a voxel goes to the tract with the most streamlines there).

50% level: the shell that held half of a healthy subject's tract on a real rescan (5 rescans of 2 adults, MASiVar).

Widest: every voxel any run gave the tract. On those rescans it held only of the rescan's tract, so even this shell misses about a third.

Reading the fans

Each fan is one seed near the tumor, drawn 17 times, once per run, colored by the tract that run gave it. A tight fan in one color is a reliable streamline. A fan that splits into routes of different colors shows where identity is decided.

Limits

Paths are stored at 20 points each and drawn as smooth curves, so fine geometry is approximate. Shell surfaces are smoothed isosurfaces of vote counts; their thresholds set the apparent boundary. The tumor is the dataset's mask registered from the T1. Coordinates are scanner millimeters, viewed in the patient's frame (R/L, A/P, S/I labels).
